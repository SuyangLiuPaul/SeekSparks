- **v1.6.32** — **the same original word, lit in every translation at once.** The word under study now draws every *other* printed occurrence of its lexical Strong's number in a green fill, across the Chinese line, the English line and the originals line simultaneously — so the reader can see that 万物 / "All things" / πάντα are one word, which is the whole reason a parallel view exists. **BibleWorks has no such feature, and the help proves the divergence is deliberate rather than an oversight on my part.** `bwh11_BrowseWindow` lists every highlight the Browse window can draw — Toggle Difference Highlighting (same-language versions only), Clear Hit Highlighting, manual phrase selection, the Diagrammer echo — and *none* of them is lexical. BibleWorks answers this question in a **pane**, not in the text: `bwh10_AnalysisWindow` says "The Use Tab shows a list of all occurrences of a word in the local context as the mouse is moved over words in the Browse Window." A list tells you *how many*; colour in the text tells you **which word over there is this word**, which is the only form of the answer that survives four parallel rows. Decisively, `bwh10l_AWUse` states "The Use Tab is currently not supported for double byte languages like Chinese" — the one reader this app exists for is the reader BibleWorks explicitly could not serve here. Four things worth carrying forward. **(1) The lit set is derived from the pane's subject, not re-decided.** `siblingStrongs()` takes the single value `_analysisWord` already resolves ("the pinned word if there is one, otherwise the last one hovered"), so the green words and the Analysis pane are *incapable* of disagreeing — there is one answer and both read it — and the echoes therefore follow the pin for free, staying lit while the reader walks the pointer off the text to read the pane. That is the entire point: a highlight that died on mouse-out would reintroduce the exact defect v1.6.28 fixed. **(2) The chapter gate needs its trailing separator.** A pin outlives navigation, so the lit set is gated on the `book|chapter` printed *now*; but `John|1` is a prefix of `John|11`, so testing the bare prefix leaks chapter 1's pin into chapters 10–19 — lighting words with no pinned word visible anywhere to explain the colour. Test it against `John|11`, not just against another book. **(3) Hue alone is not a third state.** `siblingBg` green and `selectionBg` blue measure **1.04 / 1.05 / 1.08:1** in luminance across the three palettes — for a red-green colour-blind reader those two fills are the same fill, which would have collapsed hover and echo into one. The fix is a second, non-colour channel: `wordMarkUnderline()` underlines only the reader's OWN subject (hover, pinned) and never an echo, so the three states are green-fill / blue-fill-plus-underline / blue-fill-plus-underline-plus-gold-border. It is a free function rather than three lines in the widget purely so it can be tested, since the widget cannot be. **(4) Threading the raw pointer position would have flickered, and only reasoning caught it** — no test and no screenshot would have. The Browse window threads the *latched* subject down as `hoverKey` and each word overrides it with `withHover()` only while the pointer is physically inside that word. Thread the live pointer instead and, in the 5px `Wrap` gap between two words, the latched subject matches its own `litStrongs`, falls through to `WordMark.sibling`, and every word the reader sweeps past blinks green on the way out. The rejected design is preserved as a named test so it cannot be "simplified" back in. **Data note, measured not assumed:** 2,432,524 word slots surveyed — the format is uniformly `[GH]\d+` with **no zero-padding anywhere**, so `==` is a safe comparison; 62,795 slots are empty, which is why "empty must never match empty" is its own test; and 86.6% of cuvs-plus numbers in a verse also appear in BSB's same verse, so the cross-row echo actually fires most of the time rather than being a demo-only feature. **Process:** verified by driving headless Chrome over CDP at 1280x800 and counting exact-RGB pixels rather than eyeballing screenshots — the Read tool downscales a 1280px capture to ~320px, which is far too coarse to see a 1.5px border, whereas a pixel census answers "did the echo light in N distinct row bands" objectively. One trap: a Flutter `Tooltip` overlay can *occlude* part of the browse column, so a before/after pixel delta will show phantom "new" highlights that are really just un-occlusion — diff the coordinates and look at them before believing the number.
- **v1.6.28** — **hover proposes, a click commits.** The Analysis pane followed the mouse, which meant it could not be used: hovering a tagged word filled the pane, and moving the pointer *toward* that pane crossed every word in between, so the content the reader was reaching for was gone before they arrived — unscrollable, unselectable, no click-through to a lexicon entry. The feature destroyed itself in the act of being used. BibleWorks has the identical defect and its own help admits it; `bwh10a` documents Shift-to-freeze "while you move the mouse cursor to the Word Analysis Window to use the scroll bar", and `bwh10l` adds a Freeze checkbox *and* a right-click "update only when shift key depressed" inversion. **Three escape hatches is evidence the default is wrong, not a feature set to copy.** Shift-freeze is also the wrong shape for this app: it needs a keyboard, on a build deliberately gated below 1072px so it can run on tablets *where there is no Shift key and no hover at all*; it must be held while the same hand moves the pointer; and it freezes the PANE rather than naming a WORD, so nothing on screen says what you froze — which is why BibleWorks draws no mark for it. So a click **pins**, and a pin is an *identity*, not a *mode*: it names one printed occurrence, it is drawn in the text, and while it stands the pointer may go anywhere without changing the subject. Shift-freeze stays, because it costs one boolean and a BibleWorks reader will try it out of habit. Four things worth carrying forward. **(1) The occurrence key must contain book and chapter, not just verse and index** — a pin outlives navigation, so without them pinning word 2 of verse 3 in John 1 lights up word 2 of verse 3 in John 2 the moment the reader turns the page: a gold marker on a word nobody chose, in a verse they have never seen. `versionCode` is in the key too, so the same Greek word on the originals row and on a tagged translation row are two distinct marks, which is correct. **(2) `pinMark` had to be its own palette colour.** The app's single gold `#C9A227` measures **1.54:1** against the paper theme's tan `selectionBg` — on 护眼纸质 the marker simply would not have been there. The three palettes now measure 3.98 / 6.67 / 4.13:1 and the test *computes* the ratio from sRGB luminance rather than hard-coding it, so a future palette edit that dims the marker fails the suite instead of shipping. Relatedly, every mark reserves the same 1.5px border and only the colour changes: a border that appeared on click would widen the word and reflow the line under the reader's own pointer, so the click would move the thing it had just committed to. **(3) Clicking a DIFFERENT word moves the pin rather than swallowing the tap.** This is the single most dangerous failure mode, because on a touch screen a tap is the only way to look at anything, and a pin that refused to move would strand a tablet reader on the first word they ever touched with the text apparently dead. Three exits, all verified: click the same word again, Esc, or the worded Unpin button. Nothing is pinned on first load; the empty state is untouched. **(4) Esc needs `HardwareKeyboard`, not `Shortcuts`/`Actions`,** which never fires — Flutter routes key events from the *focused* node upward and nothing is focused in a mouse-driven reading pane. The handler returns `false` so it observes without consuming, and is gated on `ModalRoute.isCurrent` so a standing pin cannot eat the Esc that should close a dialog above it. A latent bug fell out of this: `onWordTap` was passing the *workspace cursor* verse instead of the tapped word's verse, so crossRefs / stats / related / phrases / verseLists — everything routed through `_analysisVerse` — was filed under the wrong verse whenever the two differed; harmless while click was a minor gesture, load-bearing the moment click became *the* gesture. The callback now carries the whole `BrowseHover`. **Two process notes.** The state machine is a Flutter-free `AnalysisFocus` because it *had* to be: `browse_window_tagged_test` already documents that `FetchVerses.loadVerseList` never resolves under `flutter test`, so the Browse window sits on its spinner, no word ever renders, and **no widget test can reach this behaviour at all** — the wiring was verified by driving headless Chrome over the DevTools Protocol at 1280x800 (Node 24 has a built-in `WebSocket`, so zero installs). Two traps in that, both of which cost real time and produced convincing false "the fix doesn't work" results: `targets.find(t => t.type === 'page')` is *not* your app once Chrome opens an About tab, so filter targets by URL; and `Page.captureScreenshot` can return a **stale frame**, so wait ~2s after an interaction and re-shoot with no input before believing a bad result. Also: the pin opening the right pane reflows the browse column, so re-measure pixel coordinates from the current screenshot before every click instead of reusing them across a sequence.

- **v1.6.27** — **morphological search**, BibleWorks' Graphical Search Engine (bwh17), which the last research pass named the single biggest remaining gap. The reader could already see what a word *is*; it could not ask what else in the book looks like it. Now a ninth Analysis tab builds a query by clicking parts of speech and features, scoped to chapter / book / testament, seeded from the word under the pointer, over the MorphGNT (CC BY-SA 3.0) and OSHB/WLC (CC BY 4.0) tagging that was already bundled and licence-clear — no BibleWorks data anywhere near it. Three things are worth carrying forward. **(1) A Semitic word is a stack of morphemes, and it matches only if ONE morpheme satisfies EVERY constraint at once.** Asking each constraint of the word as a whole — the obvious implementation — returns 7016 "feminine verbs" in the WLC where there are 6129: 887 false positives, typically `HC/Vqv2mp/Sp3fs`, a *masculine* imperative wearing a feminine object suffix. That number is measured against the real text and pinned in a test, because the wrong answer looks entirely plausible on screen. **(2) Every offered value carries its live count, and a value that would return nothing is not offered at all** — the counts come out of the same single pass as the hits (a word lands in slot S's tally when some morpheme fails *at most* S), and the "what if I switched this one facet" promise was verified by actually re-running the switched query over Genesis and comparing totals, not asserted. **(3) The tab is APPENDED, never inserted:** `workbench.analysisTab` persists by INDEX, so reordering the enum silently moves every reader who had a tab open to a different one. Two data defects surfaced from surveying the whole corpus rather than reading the spec: 1068 Aramaic verbs were being given *Hebrew* binyan names, and OSHB's `x` "unknown or unnecessary" placeholder was being read as a value on 2684 words. The POS→slot map is likewise measured, not copied from the documentation — MorphGNT leaves personal-pronoun person blank, so `RP` genuinely has no person slot, and offering one would have produced a dead chip on every pronoun query. Performance needed no cleverness: 4037 distinct codes across 438,821 words parse in 18 ms, so memoising the parse makes every repeat query free (~100–300 ms warm across a whole testament). The pane keys on the *book*, not the verse — a query is the reader's work and must survive the pointer crossing a word — and splits into two scroll regions (facets capped at 45%, hits taking the rest) because a long facet list otherwise pushes every result below the fold, which is how the widget test found it. **Two process notes for whoever runs next.** The pure core (`morph_query.dart`, `morph_search_service.dart`, the `morphology.dart` refactor and three test files) is *in* `b21d17b`, labelled "chore(release): v1.6.26" and described by nothing — swept in by another writer's `git add -A` while it was still in progress. That is the second time; the rule in this repo is **stage exact paths by name**, and it is not optional. And this build was made from a scratch `git worktree` at the committed tree, not from the working copy: the working copy registers `assets/nasb-ev.json`, `assets/nsn-plus.json` and `assets/tagged/nsn-plus/` under its own comment saying never to deploy them, so `release_web.sh` run in place would have shipped restricted licensed texts to dev. Same reason the version bump here is staged as a single hunk of `pubspec.yaml` rather than the whole file.

- **v1.6.20** — the command line learned its **verbs** (BibleWorks bwh44, Command Line Shortcuts). v1.6.19 taught it to *search*; it could already navigate; but it still could not change what you were looking at or narrow where you were looking — which is most of what a BibleWorks reader does between searches, and all of it without touching the mouse. Now `d kjv` / `d -kjv` / `d c` / `d english` edit the Browse stack, `p kjv bsb` restacks in the order given, `l gen` / `l matt 5-7` / `l nt` scope the search and bare `l` lifts it, and a bare number is a verse in the chapter you already have open (`3:16` is chapter and verse in the book you are in). `l` is the one that closes a genuine functional hole rather than a fluency gap: `workbench_provider` carried a comment saying the workbench command line "always scans the whole Bible — book scoping lives in the standalone SearchPage", so until now the workbench literally could not search only the Gospels. Four things a naive reading of the help file misses, and they are where the work was: `d` is not one command but a **set editor with four arities** (add, remove, clear, add-a-whole-language); the **search version can never leave the display**, because BibleWorks defines `d c` as "clears all versions except search version", which makes that an invariant of the display and not a default anyone may override; `p` is not `d` three times — it carries an ORDER, which is the one thing the existing version-checkbox dialog cannot express; and a bare number is a **verse**, not a chapter, which is the opposite of what every other Bible app's jump box does. Routing the verbs *first* in `_submit` is safe for the same structural reason v1.6.19's grammar was: every verb is a single ASCII letter followed by whitespace or end-of-line, or a line made only of digits and a colon, and no bundled edition code, book name or query control character has either shape — pinned by a 19-input "not a verb" test group rather than argued, since the failure mode is silent theft of a search someone typed. Every verb echoes what it did, above the results rather than instead of them: a verb's whole effect lands in a *different pane*, which below the three-pane breakpoint is not even on screen, and `d bsb` must not throw away a hit list that took three commands to build. Refusals name the token (`d niv` → "No edition called \"niv\"" plus the list of ones we do have) because falling through to a text search for "d niv" would find nothing and teach a first-day BibleWorks migrant that the verb does not exist. Deliberately not implemented, each recorded with its reason: `d fav`/`o fav` (no favourites UI to name, and their absence removes the `d c` ambiguity), `f c`/`f u`/`f a` (no result checkboxes exist here), `t #` (the help's "current context tab" has no equivalent surface — mapping it onto the Analysis tabs would invent a meaning, and those numbers have already shifted eight times), and the command line's "never forgets" behaviour where ↑ also restores the search version of the recalled line. `l` deliberately stops at chapter granularity: verse granularity is the Verse List Manager's existing job, and its limits are already a corpus snapshot, so the versification caveat is unchanged rather than newly introduced. `parallelMode`/`parallelVersions` moved out of `workbench_page`'s State into `WorkbenchProvider` — a verb typed in the left pane has to reach state the centre pane owns, and a compiler-checked migration beats a published mirror that drifts. Logic in `utils/command_verb.dart`, Flutter-free; 70 core + 23 widget tests against a stand-in edition registry so adding a Bible cannot break the grammar; en/zh-Hans/zh-Hant throughout. One bug caught while writing the tests: the help strings shipped BibleWorks' own examples (`d nas`, `d 太`) and neither resolves here — `nasb` is the code and `太` is a *book*, not a version, so the on-screen help was teaching two commands that fail.
- **v1.6.19** — the command line finally speaks the BibleWorks query grammar (bwh16). The workbench pane has been *called* a command line since v1.0.2 while only ever doing a substring scan, so a reader arriving with BibleWorks muscle memory who typed `.love god` searched for that literal string and was told there were no results — and plain `god world` failed the same way for a different reason, because the scan strips spaces, so a two-word search could never match anything and never said why. Now: `.` AND, `/` OR, `'` lexical phrase, `;` linear phrase, `!` negation, `*`/`?` wildcards, `*N` word gaps, `;N` verse context. Three things a naive reading of the help file misses, and they are where the work was: `!` has two arities (a verse exclusion in AND/OR, but a one-word *position* inside a phrase, which is the documented `'!your *5 house` trap); `*` has two meanings (a character wildcard when glued to a word, a word gap when standing alone); and `;N` is a **verse** context, which quietly makes the window rather than the verse the unit of search. Three deliberate divergences from BibleWorks, each taken as the conservative option: a windowed hit reports *every* participating verse, not just the first, so no verse in the list lacks a search word; a NOT term excludes on the verse rather than the window, so widening a context never silently subtracts results; and the pane echoes the parse back in words ("All of: god, world"), which no competitor does — a grammar whose operators are punctuation is only safe if the reader can see what the punctuation was taken to mean, otherwise a wrong query is indistinguishable from a wrong Bible. The grammar is routed *first* in `runSearch`, which is safe precisely because control characters are first-position only, so no reference, version abbreviation or Strong's expression can be stolen from the branches below it — pinned by a test rather than argued. Highlighting had to learn the grammar too or every command result would have listed verses with nothing marked in them. Two real bugs found by testing against the actual 31,102-verse KJV rather than fixtures: `.?` matched the entire Bible (a bare metacharacter became an any-token slot; now gated on CJK presence, since English wildcards arrive as their own whitespace-delimited piece), and a pointed Hebrew query folded at parse time was compared against unfolded search keys, losing every hit before tokenization. Performance verified on real data, not assumed: `.melchizedek` tokenizes 2 of 31,102 verses; the worst realistic query `.lord god israel*;10` runs in 121ms, which is why the engine is synchronous rather than paying for an isolate hop. Also worth recording: the KJV spells the NT occurrences "Melchisedec", so that search returns 2 verses and not 9 — every corpus figure in the tests was measured, none invented. Logic in `utils/command_query.dart`, Flutter-free; 56 core + 13 real-corpus + 19 widget tests, en/zh-Hans/zh-Hant throughout.

- **v1.6.15** — Phrase Matching (BibleWorks bwh51) as the seventh Analysis tab. Related Verses shipped two days earlier asks which verses share the most *words*; this asks which repeat the same *sequence*, because a bag of words cannot tell "thus says the LORD of hosts" from a verse that merely contains those five words scattered about — and finding who *quotes* a verse is a question only word order can answer. The gap is the feature rather than a tolerance: 1..G words may be inserted between phrase words, which is what lets Matthew's "and they shall call" still read as Isaiah's "and shall call", and G bounds each step rather than the whole phrase so a 3-word phrase cannot spread across half a verse. A dropped word is allowed too, but only an interior one — dropping an end word would silently turn a length-3 phrase into a length-2 one. Chopping a verse into 3-word windows leaves mostly filler and BibleWorks offers no answer to that, so phrases in more than 1% of what was scanned start unchecked with their verse count on the chip; Accordance's ">50% common words" rule was rejected because it discards "a virgin shall", which is the whole point of Isaiah 7:14.
